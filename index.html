Why WASP-121 b is a benchmark ultra-hot Jupiter

At 2409 K equilibrium temperature and orbiting so close its shape is measurably tidally distorted, WASP-121 b sits at the extreme end of hot-Jupiter irradiation. Earlier HST work (Evans et al. 2017, 2018) found evidence for a stratospheric temperature inversion and metal oxide absorption (vanadium oxide) on its dayside — the atmospheric equivalent of Earth's ozone layer, but from a completely different chemistry driven by extreme heat rather than UV-absorbing ozone.

More recent JWST phase-curve spectroscopy (the data behind this report) tracks how the planet's emitted spectrum changes continuously as different hemispheres rotate into view across a full orbit, directly measuring how efficiently (or poorly) the atmosphere moves heat from the permanently irradiated dayside to the nightside.

Flux-space phase curves and temperature-space contrast

The top panel keeps NRS1 and NRS2 separate and averages the released planet/star flux ratios within each detector band at every phase. A first harmonic is overlaid only as a compact descriptive summary. The lower panels compare brightness-temperature spectra in phase windows nearest eclipse and transit. This separation matters: flux ratio is the measured observable, whereas brightness temperature is a wavelength-dependent transformation and its average is not an energy-budget temperature.

The first-harmonic conjunction estimates are 3915/158 ppm (NRS1) and 4910/620 ppm (NRS2), closely tracking the published broadband values from the same observation. This is a reproducibility cross-check, not independent confirmation. Reduced χ² values of 14.36 and 13.07 show that a first harmonic and diagonal channel errors are not adequate for inference; the peak locations therefore have no new physical uncertainty or detection significance attached here. The 1493.2 ± 3.8 K wavelength-averaged monochromatic contrast is likewise not a bolometric hemisphere temperature. For energy-budget work, use an analysis that models unobserved wavelengths, such as the cited NIRISS/SOSS result (2717 ± 17 K dayside, 1562 ± 19 K nightside).

Data and method notes

System parameters come from the NASA Exoplanet Archive TAP service. Seven numerical products are pinned to Zenodo v8 (record 10.5281/zenodo.20651891): phase and wavelength grids, flux-ratio values/errors, and brightness-temperature values with asymmetric errors. See source provenance, the analysis, machine-readable summary, and derived phase curves. Source identities and matrix shapes are checked by scripts/validate_data.py. Inter-channel covariance is unavailable; diagonal formal errors are not treated as physical parameter uncertainties.
